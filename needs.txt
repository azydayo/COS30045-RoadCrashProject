1. **Structural Gap in Road Safety:** This is the most valuable insight from this project regarding social equity. **Urban-Rural Safety Disparities:** By comparing the proportion of hospitalizations in large cities, remote areas, and extremely remote areas, the impact of geographical environment on the consequences of traffic accidents can be identified. **Ethnic Health Disparities:** By comparing annual hospitalization data and exponential growth rates for First Nations and non-Indigenous peoples, the vulnerability of specific ethnic groups in road safety can be quantified over a decade. 2. **Vulnerability Profiling:** Using segmented data, it is possible to accurately identify which groups are most in need of policy intervention. **Evolution of High-Risk Users:** By analyzing the percentage changes of car drivers, motorcyclists, and cyclists in total hospitalizations, the changes in risk exposure for various road users over time can be determined. **Age-Based Risk Patterns:** Using heatmaps to show the distribution of hospitalizations by year and age group, peak injury periods can be identified for specific age groups, such as "teenage drivers" or "elderly pedestrians." 3. Healthcare Impact: This dimension focuses on the long-term impact of accidents on socioeconomic and public health resources. Changes in average injury severity: By analyzing trends in average length of hospital stay per case, it can be concluded whether the severity of injuries (recovery time) from a single collision is increasing, even if the total number of accidents may be decreasing. Healthcare resource utilization: Combining annual trends in total bed days quantifies the cumulative pressure of road accidents on the Australian hospital system. 4.
